# Decisions made while building v0.2

Where DESIGN.md v0.2 was silent or ambiguous, the build agents chose the option closest to it and recorded the choice here, one numbered bullet per choice, so it can be confirmed or changed. Deviations from DESIGN.md are marked as such and noted in DESIGN.md too. Milestones 9 onwards.

## Milestone 10, first step: layouts, the default, per-member copies (migrations 0023 and 0024)

1. **Spaces outside any section** at migration time went into a section called "Spaces", first in the default layout, since that is where the old index showed them. It only exists on forums that had such spaces.
2. **The "Feeds" section is known by its name.** A member's copy of the default keeps the member's own section named "Feeds" (lowest position if there are several), with what is in it, and puts it last. The copy skips default sections named "Feeds" and instead adds whatever they hold to the member's Feeds section. A migrated default has no Feeds section; the setup wizard's does, empty, as §3.5 says.
3. **"Members who haven't edited theirs"** are those whose layout has `edited_at` null. Every change to the default copies it again into those layouts (`layout_copy_default` in the migration, called from `crate::layouts` after each default write). Adding, renaming, moving or deleting a section, and following, unfollowing or moving a space, marks a member's layout as edited; muting does not, so a member who only mutes keeps following the default.
4. **A space is in at most one section of a layout** (a trigger enforces it). Following a space that is already in another section of the layout moves it there.
5. **Essential spaces** are shown in one block at the top of the front page, headed "Spaces", in their order on the viewer's layout and then by name, and are left out of the sections below so nothing appears twice. Sections with nothing to show (empty, or holding only spaces the viewer can't read, has muted, or that are archived) are not rendered. An empty front page says so and links to `/spaces`.
6. **Muting** takes the space out of the member's sections, as §3.5 says, whether or not they have edited their layout, and a later copy of the default into an unedited layout leaves the muted space out (migration 0024; the first version of the copy put it back, hidden only by the front page's filter). Unmuting does not put a space back in any section, whether or not the member follows the default: following it does, and following a muted space unmutes it. (A layout that follows the default is the default less what its owner has muted now, so a member who follows the default and unmutes a space does see it again the next time the default changes; one who has edited theirs doesn't.) Making a space essential unmutes it for everyone; the store and a trigger both refuse to mute an essential space.
7. **Positions.** Sections keep whatever position they are given and sort by `(position, id)`, so an editor can number them as it likes; a space is placed at an index within its section and the section's spaces are renumbered from 0. New sections and followed spaces go last.
8. **Setup's first spaces.** Announcements and General are readable by everyone (a forum's front door should have something on it; §3.1's members-only default for *new topic spaces* is left as it is), replied to by members, started by staff (Announcements) or members (General), administered by staff rather than by the first admin personally, created by the admin, and essential. If a slug is already taken (the database wasn't quite empty), that space is used instead of making one.
9. **The admin pages.** `/admin/spaces` lists spaces by the default layout's sections, then "Not on the default front page", marking essential and archived ones, and keeps its forms for adding, renaming, moving and deleting the default layout's sections at `/admin/sections…` until `/admin/layout` arrives in the next step. A space's settings page gives staff a "Section on the default front page" select ("Not on it" takes the space off; choosing a section appends it there; the old position field is gone, ordering being the layout editor's job) and admins an "Essential" box. Staff doing either to a space they don't administer in their own right is logged (`edit_space`, `mark_space_essential`, `unmark_space_essential`).
10. **Breadcrumbs** on space and thread pages no longer name a section, since sections are each member's own.
11. **Deleting an account** deletes the member's layout and mutes along with everything else that is theirs alone.
12. **The default layout row is made on demand** if missing (tests empty the tables with `TRUNCATE … CASCADE`); a partial unique index keeps there from being two. Writers to a layout take its row lock, and copying the default into a member's layout takes that layout's lock too, so two admins saving at once (or an admin's save and a member's edit) don't trip over each other's copying; a new account's copy waits for any change to the default in progress. Propagating a change to the default lists the followers once and then, for each, locks their row and checks again that `edited_at` is still null (migration 0024): a member's first edit committed while the propagation waited for their row is kept, and their layout is theirs from then on.
13. **Tests.** The fixture's `Forum::space` puts each space in a default-layout section (the one given, or a catch-all "Spaces" section that comes first) so that existing tests about the front page keep meaning what they did; `Forum::unlisted_space` makes a space on nobody's front page. The access-layer property model tracks each member's layout, mutes and the essential flag, and checks the front page against them.
14. **The data export** (§2) includes the member's front page as they have arranged it, under `front_page` (`edited_at`, and the sections in order, each with the slugs of its spaces, whether or not they can read them now, since the arrangement is theirs), and the slugs of the spaces they have muted under `muted_spaces`.
15. **The moderation log** says "made Games essential" and "made Games no longer essential" for the two essential actions, the space's name before the complement, as a member would say it.
16. **Left for later steps** of milestone 10, after the second step below: `/settings/layout` and the editor island with its plain form, and `/admin/layout` (done in the third step, bullets 26 onwards).

## Milestone 10, second step: the `/latest` tabs, unread counts, mutes, Follow and Mute forms

17. **The Front page tab** (`/latest/front`) lists the threads in every space the front page shows: the essential spaces and the spaces in the viewer's sections. §3.5 said "every space in your sections"; essential spaces are on the front page whatever the sections hold, so a tab called "Front page" without them would be odd (the setup wizard's forum would have an empty one). §3.5 now says so. Archived spaces are left out, as the front page leaves them out; All keeps them, as it did.
18. **A section's tab** (`/latest/s/{id}`) lists the threads in that section's spaces as the layout has them, essential spaces included, readable, not archived and not muted. Tabs are shown for All, Front page (members only) and every section with at least one space the viewer can see, plus the section being looked at (its page works even when there is nothing to show). The section must belong to the viewer's layout (the default layout's sections for a visitor); anyone else's, or nonsense, is not found. A visitor asking for `/latest/front` is sent to `/latest`, since they have no front page of their own and the design gives them only All and the default's sections. Each tab has one address (`?page=1` and padded ids redirect), so visitors' tabs stay in the anonymous cache under bounded keys, tagged with the spaces they cover and `site` (the default layout).
19. **Mutes** leave a space out of the All tab (and so the index's "Latest" list), the Front page and section tabs, the default view of `/spaces`, and search results, unless the search names that space with `in=`, in which case the member has asked for it. `/spaces?muted=1` shows a member's muted spaces, flagged "Muted"; the link to it appears once they have muted something, and for visitors `muted=1` is just another spelling of `/spaces`. The muted space's own page says it is muted and what that means. The All tab's note says it leaves out the spaces the member has muted, when they have.
20. **Follow, Unfollow, Mute and Unmute** are plain forms in the space page's toolbar, for members who can read the space. Follow puts the space at the end of the first section of the member's layout (by position, whatever it is called, Feeds included), or makes a section called "Following" when the layout has none; the toolbar then says which section it is in. Following and unfollowing mark the layout edited, as any arrangement does (bullet 3); muting doesn't. An essential space offers no Mute and refuses one with a 403. A suspended member can do none of these; a visitor is sent to log in.
21. **Unread counts** on the front page count a space's threads with posts past the member's read marker (a thread never opened counts, as prototype decision 10 says), shown as "N unread" beside the space's name; visitors see none.
22. **"Mark all read" per section** is `POST /latest/s/{id}/read`, offered on the section's tab and in the section's heading on the front page (whose form says `next=/` to come back there). It moves the member's read markers to the end of every thread they can read in the section's spaces that the tab would show. It works only on the member's own sections.
23. **Query budgets.** A visitor's `/latest` is now four queries instead of three: the default layout's sections make the tabs. A member's stays at five: whether they have muted anything comes back with their sections.
24. **Wording.** The watch form's "of nothing (muted)" became "of nothing", so that "mute" means one thing, the per-space mute, and the notifications page's foot says "Choose how closely to watch a thread or space from its page".
25. **A member's layout writes never reach the default.** Follow and Unfollow find the member's layout with `layouts::own_id`, which makes them a copy of the default first if they somehow have none (every account is given one at creation, so this is belt and braces), rather than with `layouts::id_for`, whose fallback to the default is for reading a front page and would otherwise have put a member's change into the default and, from there, into every follower's front page. `id_for` is documented as read-only.

## Milestone 10, third step: the editors, and the default's propagation

26. **One editor, two layouts.** `/settings/layout` (the member's own) and `/admin/layout` (the default, admins only) are the same page and forms over a different layout, with the island's endpoints at `/api/layout` and `/api/admin/layout`. The member's editor is open to any logged-in member to look at; a suspended member can change nothing (403), and a visitor is sent to log in. Writes always go to the member's own layout (`layouts::own_id`), never the default.
27. **The plain forms** are: add a section (at the end), rename it, move it up or down (swapping with its neighbour; nothing at either end), delete it (a confirmation page naming its spaces first), add a space to a section (its "Add a space" choice offers the spaces in no section, then those in other sections as "Name (in Section)", which moves them), move a space up or down within its section, and take it off the front page. Each redirects back with a notice (`?done=section|section-deleted|saved|unmuted|applied`). The old position number fields are gone.
28. **What the editor shows.** Only spaces the viewer can read, in sections and to add; archived spaces in a section are shown, flagged, so they can be tidied away, but aren't offered to add. A member's muted spaces aren't offered to add (unmute first); their list of mutes is on the page with Unmute forms that come back to it (`/c/{slug}/unmute` takes an optional `next`). Spaces the viewer can't see keep their places: "up" puts a space before the previous space the viewer can see (or at the top), "down" after the next one (or at the end), and the island's index means "before the space the viewer sees at this index, counting without the one being moved" (none, or past the end, means last).
29. **The island** replaces the plain forms (sections, spaces, add-section form) but not the mutes list or the admin's "apply" form, which stay plain. It drags spaces within a section, between sections, into a section from the list of spaces not on the front page, and onto that list to take them off; each space also has Move up/Move down buttons and a "Move to…" choice (including "Off the front page"), so nothing needs dragging on a touch screen or keyboard. Sections are renamed in place, moved with buttons, and deleted after an inline "Delete the section / Keep it". Each change is saved as it is made, and the server answers with the layout as it now is (`{"layout": …}`), which the island shows; refusals come back as `{"error": …}` and are shown above the editor. Unknown `/api/…` addresses now answer with a JSON 404.
30. **Editing makes the layout the member's own**, as before (bullet 3), whether through the forms or the island; a step that would change nothing (moving the top section up, renaming a section to the name it has, dropping a space where it already is, sending the sections' order as it stands) isn't a write, so it neither makes the layout the member's own nor, for the default, copies it out again and clears the cached pages. Muting and unmuting still aren't edits.
31. **"Apply to members who haven't edited theirs"** copies the default again into every layout with `edited_at` null. Since every change to the default already reaches them as it is made (bullet 3), what this adds is putting back spaces a follower has unmuted since the last change, and catching up anything changed outside the editor. The admin's page says how many members follow the default.
32. **The essential flag** is set from the default's editor (per space, in its sections and in the list of spaces not on it) as well as from the space's settings. Marking a space essential or not is always recorded in the moderation log now, from either place, since it is an admin's power over every front page and never part of running the space; the space's other settings are still logged only when staff change a space they don't administer in their own right.
33. **`/admin/spaces`** now only lists every space the admin can read, by name, with its flags and who can read and post, leading to each space's settings and to the editor; the admin navigation has "Default front page". The `/admin/sections…` routes and their template are gone (404). The setup wizard's last step points at `/admin/layout`.
34. **Links to the editor**: the member's front page ends with "Arrange your front page", and the account settings page has a "Your front page" panel.
35. **Browser tests.** The seed has six more members who post nothing (Alma, Bram, Cleo, Dara, Emil, Fern), one per Playwright project to arrange the front page of, and two more admins (Gwen, Hal) for the default's editor, so that these tests neither share login rate limits nor disturb the front pages other tests look at. The admin's test makes a space of its own and only adds to the default what it takes away again. With JavaScript, drags are sent as the browser's drag events; at 320px the island's "Move to" choice is used for one step instead.


## Milestone 11: thread subscriptions, fewer kinds (migration 0025)

36. **The kinds** are stored as `mention`, `subscription` (a new post in a thread the member is subscribed to), `message` and `moderation` (a notice about the member, shown "From the moderators"), and a check constraint allows only these. When one post concerns a member in two ways they get one notification: a message in a conversation, otherwise a mention rather than a subscription. A mention reaches a member whether or not they are subscribed. Quoting someone's post no longer tells them anything; mentioning them does.
37. **Stored notifications of the old kinds** (`reply`, `watched_thread`, `watched_space`) were kept, as `subscription`, rather than deleted, so nobody loses their history. All three were about a new post in a thread, and `subscription` reads "Ruth posted in “Brass”", which is true of each, so no row says something false.
38. **Conversations use subscriptions too**, since a DM is a thread (§1, principle 6) and the old Mute on a conversation needed somewhere to live. Everyone in a conversation is subscribed when it starts or when they are added (or added back), leaving unsubscribes them, and a message notifies the people in it who are subscribed. Someone in it who turned its notifications off still hears of a message that @-mentions them, as a mention (bullet 36); someone who has left it hears nothing, since they can no longer read it. The conversation's Mute/Unmute became "Turn off notifications" / "Turn on notifications", so that "mute" keeps meaning the per-space mute (bullet 24). Nobody can subscribe to a conversation they aren't in: staff reading a reported conversation get a 404 from the toggle. §9 now says so.
39. **Migrating watches.** A thread watch at `watching` became a subscription. Thread watches at `normal` or `muted` were dropped: a member who had muted a thread was never subscribed to it, so they now hear only of mentions there. Everyone still in a conversation was subscribed to it unless they had muted it. Space watches were dropped, whatever their level.
40. **The one-time notice** went to every member (not deleted accounts) who had a watch on any space, watching or muted, since both stopped working. It is an in-app `moderation` notification with no actor, made by the migration, and not emailed (the migration queues no email jobs; it is news about the forum, not something that needs them back): "Notifications have changed: spaces can no longer be watched or muted for notifications, so your choices for spaces have been removed. You now hear about mentions of you, new posts in threads you are subscribed to, and messages. You are subscribed to the threads you start, and you can subscribe to any other thread, or unsubscribe, from its page."
41. **Who is subscribed.** `store::create_thread` subscribes the author, so every way a thread is started (the form, an approved held thread, the seed, the test fixtures) does it. A thread made by a moderator moving posts into it subscribes nobody, since nobody started it, and merging threads doesn't carry subscriptions over: §9 says nothing else subscribes anyone.
42. **Mutes and notifications.** Muting a space takes it off the member's lists (§3.5) and no longer has anything to do with notifications: a mention there, or a new post in a thread there they are subscribed to, still reaches them. ("Following a space means it is on your front page and nothing else", and muting is its counterpart.)
43. **The toggle** is `POST /t/{id}/subscribe` and `POST /t/{id}/unsubscribe`, for any logged-in member who can read the thread (a suspended member included, for what they can still read) or is in the conversation; anything else is a 404, and visitors are sent to log in. Subscribing twice is the same as once. The thread page's toolbar shows "Subscribe", or "Unsubscribe" with "Subscribed: you hear about new posts". The space page has nothing about notifications. Thread slugs can't be `subscribe` or `unsubscribe` (the migration renamed any that were, adding `-thread`); `watch` is no longer reserved.
44. **Email.** The preferences are mentions, threads you subscribe to, messages and notes from the moderators; the stored `watching` choice became `subscriptions` and `replies` was dropped. Unsubscribe links already sent that name `watching` or `replies` still work (checked against the name in the link) and turn off emails about subscribed threads, the closest there is now. A pending email leaves out new posts in threads, and messages in conversations, that the member has since unsubscribed from, as it used to leave out muted ones.
45. **Role changes** now send the member a notice (§9): "An admin made you a moderator.", "An admin made you an admin.", "An admin made you a moderator: you are no longer an admin.", "An admin made you a member again: you are no longer a moderator." It can't be contested, isn't sent when an admin changes their own role, and isn't sent when the role chosen is the one they had.
46. **The data export** lists `thread_subscriptions` (the thread's id, and its title if the member may read it now) in place of `watches`.
47. **The seed's** Priya subscribes to the September thread (Sam started it) so that she has notifications about it, as she used to by watching it.

## Milestone 12: lists (migration 0026)

48. **Lists are groups with an owner**, in the same `groups` table. Site groups keep their short name (slug) and a name unique among site groups; a list has no slug, and its name is unique only among its owner's lists (so every member can have a "Friends"). Names are 1 to 60 characters with spaces tidied, like site groups'.
49. **A list lets its owner in.** Wherever a grant names a list, its owner gets through as the people on it do (`member_group_ids` in migration 0026, which `UserCtx.group_ids` comes from), so that "Visible to: alice's Friends" includes alice, as a friends-only post always did. The owner is never one of its members: they aren't shown on it, and adding themselves is refused ("You don’t need to be on your own list…"). §3.3 said `group_ids` holds the groups and lists the user is "in"; it now says this too.
50. **The Friends list** is made by a trigger on `users`, so every way an account is made (setup, invites, the seed, the test fixtures) gets one; the migration gave one to every member whose account wasn't deleted. It is an ordinary list: it can be renamed, and deleted if no grant names it.
51. **How lists are named to people** (§3.3). To its owner and the people on it, a list is "Ruth’s Friends (12)": the owner's display name, the list's name, and how many people are on it (not counting the owner). To anyone else who can see a grant naming it, it is "people Ruth has chosen", once per owner however many of their lists the grant names. A space's audience label lists the site groups as before ("Organisers group"), then the lists, joined with commas and "and", with a capital at the start. The labels are made per viewer in SQL (`site_group_names`, `list_labels`); `space_targets` no longer carries group names. They appear wherever a space's audience did: the front page, the space index, the space page, the fork and move choices, the moderation queue and the admin's list of spaces. The thread page shows no audience: §3.4 says a thread that simply inherits its space's audience "shows nothing extra", and shows the lock and the named audience only when the thread is narrower than its space, which per-thread restrictions bring in milestone 14. §3.3's "on any space or thread whose grant names it" is read accordingly: a thread's own grant is its restriction.
52. **Who names a list in a grant.** Grant forms offer, for every verb, the site groups, the editor's own lists ("Your Friends") and the lists they are on ("Ruth’s Book club"): §3.3 says both sorts of group "can be named in any grant" and that only a list's owner and the people on it know it exists, so those are the people who can name it (bullet 66). A list someone else owns that the editor isn't on, but that a grant already names, is offered in that grant only (not under the other verbs), as "People Ruth has chosen" (bullet 67), so that saving the form keeps it, or unticking it takes it out, without the editor learning more; once out, only its owner or someone on it can put it back. Each verb's ticked choices are checked against what that verb offers. (A version between these let only the owner name a list, reading §3.3's "the owner alone adds and removes members" as covering grants too; bullet 66 says why it was reversed.)
53. **Admins see no lists.** `/admin/groups` and every page and form under it, and the invite form, know only site groups: a list's id there is not found, and an invite can't add people to a list (the migration took any list out of existing invites, though there could be none). Staff reach a list's own page no more than anyone else not on it.
54. **The pages.** `/settings/lists` lists the member's own lists (with how many are on each), a form to make one, and the lists they are on, as "Ruth’s Book club" with who else is on it. `/settings/lists/{id}` is, for its owner, who is on it with Remove, "Add someone" by username (with or without @; anyone whose account exists; adding someone already on it is fine), Rename, the spaces they can read whose grants name it, and Delete; for someone on it, the same without the forms. Anyone else, staff included, gets "not found" for the page and every form; someone on it who isn't the owner gets "not allowed" for the forms; a suspended member can look but not change; visitors are sent to log in. The account settings page links to it. There is no "leave a list": §3.3 says the owner alone adds and removes members.
55. **Nobody is told** they were put on a list or taken off one (§3.3's proposal).
56. **Deleting** a list is offered only while no space's grants name it; otherwise the page says "The list can’t be deleted while N spaces name it. You can rename it, or take everyone off it, instead." (the count includes spaces the owner can't read). Under "Where it is named" the owner sees the spaces they can read by name and, if there are others, "A space you can’t read names it too. Only that space’s administrators can take the list out of its settings." (or the plural), so that they know why it can't be deleted and whom to ask; the list was named there by them or by someone on it, perhaps before someone changed who reads the space. The people on the list see only the spaces they can read that name it, and nothing about any others. A grant naming it arriving at the same moment is caught by the existing trigger.
57. **Freezing.** Deleting an account, by the member or as a spammer, freezes the lists it owned (a trigger on `users.deleted_at`). A trigger refuses to add anyone to a frozen list or take anyone off it, except that someone whose own account is deleted leaves it, as account deletion already takes them out of every group. Grants naming a frozen list keep working. Its page says, to the people on it, that the owner's account was deleted and nobody can change who is on it; nobody can rename or delete it, since it has no owner who can log in.
58. **Taking someone off a list** ends their access at once, but, as §3.3's main text says, they keep the ability to see and edit their own posts' revisions (its bracketed proposal, that they lose the whole thread, was the first version built and was reversed). The server can't tell why someone can no longer read a thread, so this holds for an author's posts in any space's thread they can no longer read: taken off a list or a site group, dropped from a grant, or the thread moved. Their post's history (`/p/{id}/revisions`) and its edit form (`/p/{id}/edit`) stay open to them, and `/p/{id}` leads them to the history, since the thread isn't theirs to see. These pages show the post, its versions and who made them, and nothing else of the thread: not its title, its space or anyone else's posts; the history page says "You can no longer read the thread this post is in, but it is still yours: you can see its history and edit it." Saving an edit leads back there. The rest is as for any post of theirs: not once it is deleted or hidden, or its thread deleted; no editing in a locked thread or while silenced; and nothing while they are suspended, since a suspended member reads only what visitors read. Conversations are not included: someone who left one chose to (§3.9). Deleting such a post isn't offered (§3.3 says "see and edit"); editing it is how to change what it says. Nothing lists these posts for them; they are reached from their address, as in notifications and emails, and are in the data export.
59. **Rate limit.** Making lists allows 20 at once, then one every five minutes (§7 lists it with spaces; lists cost less and only their people see them).
60. **The data export** has `groups` (site groups only, as before), `lists` (the member's own, each with the usernames on it) and `lists_you_are_on` (each list's name and its owner's username).
61. **The notification model** in the property tests (milestone 11) only considered the people in a conversation for mentions; the code tells anyone mentioned who can read the message, which includes staff reading a reported conversation (§9: "someone @-mentioned you in a post you can read"). The model now follows the code and §9.
62. **Browser test.** Each project's list owner is the member whose front page the layout editor's tests arrange (lists don't touch front pages), and the member it puts on the list is that project's other member.
63. **Hidden posts aren't emailed.** A notification email carries a post's text only while the post is live. Staff see a hidden post on the site, marked as hidden, so that they can moderate it, but the access layer gave them its text for the email too, so a moderator mentioned in a post hidden before the email went was sent it, unmarked. The property tests found this while checking the milestone's fixes; the email now leaves it out for everyone, as it already did for members.
64. **The audience page.** `/c/{slug}/audience` ("Who can take part in …") is the "who can read this" view for a space, linked from the space page's toolbar ("Who can take part") for logged-in members. It has a panel per grant (who can read, reply, start threads, and run it) saying "Anyone, including visitors who haven’t logged in.", "Every member.", "The moderators and admins." or, for a grant naming nobody, "Nobody." (for running it: "Nobody is named, so the moderators and admins run it until someone is."); otherwise the site groups named ("Organisers group"), then the lists, then the people named one by one. A list the viewer owns or is on is "Ruth’s Book club" with who is on it; any other is "People Ruth has chosen", once per owner, with nobody named. §3.3 says site groups are visible to all members but not who is in them, so a site group's members are listed only to the people in it, as a list's are. The page says that replying and starting also need read, and an archived space says nobody can post in it. It is for members who can read the space (anyone else gets "not found", visitors are asked to log in), so it is rendered for each viewer and never cached, and its labels need no cache tags. The thread page doesn't have one: a thread has its space's audience until milestone 14 brings `/t/{id}/audience` with the per-thread restriction.
65. **Where audiences are shown.** The step that followed milestone 12 asked for the audience on the thread page and beside the reply box as well. §3.4 says a thread that simply inherits its space's audience "shows nothing extra" there, and every thread inherits until milestone 14, so these still show nothing (bullet 51); the new-thread page names the destination's audience as before, and so do the fork and move choices and the moderation queue.
66. **Grant forms offer the lists the editor is on.** The step that followed milestone 12 asked for grant forms to offer the lists the editor owns or is on, and never other people's hidden lists. Its first version kept bullet 52's earlier rule that only the owner names a list, but §3.3 doesn't say that: the owner alone decides who is on a list, and naming it in a grant changes nobody's membership, any more than naming each of its people one by one would (which anyone running a space can do). So someone on a list may now name it, for any verb, in any space they run, and the people on it are let in as for the owner's own grants. The cost falls on the owner, who can't delete a list a grant names: its page says how many spaces they can't read name it and that only those spaces' administrators can take it out (bullet 56), and they can still rename or empty it. A frozen list (bullet 57) is offered to the people on it like any other: grants naming it keep working, and its membership can no longer change.
67. **One choice per owner for lists the editor isn't on.** In a grant form, the lists a grant names that the editor neither owns nor is on are one checkbox per owner, "People Ruth has chosen", as everywhere else (bullet 51), and the form doesn't give their ids: its value is `owner:` and the owner's user id, which the server turns back into those of that owner's lists the grant names and the editor doesn't know, keeping them all while it is ticked and taking them all out when it isn't. So the form says neither which lists they are nor how many. Choices are offered per grant: a form can tick only what that grant's form offered, and a list's id sent in place of `owner:` is refused like any other choice not offered ("Choose groups from the list.").

## Milestone 13: feeds (migration 0027)

68. **Feeds are made by a trigger** on `users`, as Friends lists are (bullet 50), so every way an account is made (setup, invites, the seed, the test fixtures) gets one; the migration gave one to every member whose account wasn't deleted, and deleted accounts got none. A feed's `created_by` is its owner and its `created_at` their account's, so that the index's "Made" date is when the feed came to exist. Its grants are §3.2's defaults: `read` members, `reply` members, `start` listed (the owner), `administer` listed (the owner).
69. **A feed's address is the username in lower case.** Usernames may hold capitals and underscores and topic spaces' addresses can't, so a feed's slug is the username in lower case and may hold underscores, which only feeds' slugs may (a check constraint, the settings' tidying and `/c/{slug}`'s shape check all allow them for feeds alone). It follows the username wherever it changes (a trigger; today only deleting an account changes one). Its name is the display name, and follows the display name for as long as the two are the same: a feed its owner has renamed keeps its name.
70. **The owner holds every permission on their feed** (§2: "The owner holds all of its permissions"; §3.2). Each of its grants must let every member (or, for reading, everyone) through, or name the owner. The store adds the owner to any grant that names people (so the settings form, which doesn't show them there, can't leave them out), and refuses "only moderators and admins" for running it with "The owner of a feed always runs it." and for reading, replying or starting threads with "The owner of a feed can always read it, reply in it and start threads in it."; a trigger refuses anything else that would leave them out. The form offers "only moderators and admins" for none of the four, nor a new address ("Address: /c/ruth. A feed is always at its owner’s username."); a different address sent anyway is refused with "A feed’s address is its owner’s username." Its hints say "You always run your feed." and, under reading, replying and starting threads, "You always can." to the owner, and "Ruth always runs their feed." and "Ruth always can." to anyone else who runs it. Within that, every grant is ordinary: the owner can open starting threads to members (a guestbook) or let only their Friends read. (The first version held only `administer` to this, so an owner could take themselves off the read grant and with it lose their feed and its settings page, which only staff could then undo; migration 0028 made the rule cover every grant and mended any grant saved before it, adding the owner where a grant named people and making "only moderators and admins" the owner alone.)
71. **"A feed with no threads"** is one with no thread that isn't deleted, whoever is looking (until milestone 14 every thread in a space has the space's audience). Such a feed is left out of the space index (its owner's view included: §3.5 says "Feeds without threads are left out", and the owner has it in their Feeds section), the essential block and sections of every front page but its owner's, the counts that decide which section tabs `/latest` shows, the layout editor's spaces to add (except in the owner's own layout), the search page's choice of spaces and `/admin/spaces`. Following one isn't refused: it then sits in the member's section, shown in their layout editor so that they can take it out, but not on their front page. Its own page, `/c/{slug}`, works whether or not it has threads, for whoever can read it, and lists of where the viewer may start a thread (the new-thread and fork choices, moving threads) aren't filtered, since another member's feed is offered there only if its grants let the viewer start threads in it.
72. **Cached pages and a feed coming into sight.** The pages that leave out a feed without threads (the front page, the index, the tabs) are tagged `site`. A new thread's first post in a feed, and a thread deleted, restored or moved into or out of one, clear `site` when the feed has at most one live thread afterwards: that is when it may just have come into sight or gone out of it, and a busy feed doesn't clear every cached page with each new thread.
73. **The Feeds section.** A new member's layout is made with their feed at the end of its Feeds section (`layout_add_own_feed`, after the copy of the default); copies of the default keep the Feeds section and what is in it, so it stays there until the member moves it, takes it out or mutes it. The migration put each existing member's feed at the end of their first section called "Feeds", making one at the end of their front page if they had deleted or renamed theirs (an edited layout is the member's own, but §3.2 says the owner always sees where to post, and one new section is the least change that does it). Because a member always has their feed, `/latest` shows a tab for their Feeds section, and the front page still says "There are no spaces yet." (with "Make the first one.") when the only thing on it is their own feed waiting for its first thread.
74. **The namespace.** Joining with a username that is a space's address (in any capitals) is refused as "That username is taken.", since the new member's feed goes at that address (§3.1). The migration moved each topic space at a live member's username to the address with "-space" added, or "-space-2", "-space-3"… if that was taken, and told the admins, the space's maker and the people its administer grant named (of these, migration 0028 left the notice only with those who can read the space: bullet 80), with a notice from the moderators ("Every member now has a space of their own, a feed, at their username, so the space “Games” has moved from /c/games to /c/games-space, since games is a member’s username. Links to the old address now lead to their own feed."). The moderation log needs an actor, which a migration hasn't, so the notice is the record. Spaces at retired usernames were left alone: nobody gets a feed there.
75. **Deleting the owner's account** archives their feed (a trigger on `users`, so deleting as a spammer does it too) and takes their name off it, as off everything else: its address becomes the account's new name (`/c/deleted_12`), its name "Deleted member", and its description goes. It stays readable by whoever could read it. Nobody can open it again, staff included (the store refuses, and its settings page says it is archived for good), since nobody owns it now.
76. **A feed can't be deleted**, even before its first thread ("A feed can’t be deleted. Archive it instead."): every member has one. Its owner and staff can archive it and open it again, as administrators of any space can.
77. **Where feeds show.** A feed's page says "Your feed." to its owner, with "Your feed joins the list of spaces, and other members’ front pages, once you start one." while it has no threads, and "The feed of Ruth." (linking to her profile) to everyone else. A profile has a "Feed" line linking to the member's feed for viewers who can read it. `/spaces` marks feeds "Feed", and its toolbar offers "Showing all · topic spaces · members’ feeds" (`?kind=topic`, `?kind=feed`; `?kind=both` and other spellings redirect to the one address for each view, and an unknown or repeated `kind` is not found).
78. **Property tests.** The access machine now has every member's feed among its spaces from the start (with its grants, given random settings like any space, and the owner's Feeds section, which copies of the default keep), checks the front page, the section tabs, the editor's spaces to add and the space index (now for each kind) against the invisibility rule, and checks after every step that each of a feed's grants lets its owner through and that they can open it, run it, and (unless silenced) start threads and reply in it (bullet 70); reading a feed whose read grant names its owner is modelled too. The page machine has Ruth's feed open to visitors, on the default front page and named with a token: no viewer may see it on `/`, `/spaces` or `/spaces?kind=feed` while it has no threads, and a rule that gives it its first thread or takes its threads away checks the cached pages at once.
79. **Setup keeps the first spaces' addresses.** The setup wizard is about to make `/c/announcements` and `/c/general`, and the admin's feed goes at their username, so setup refuses either as the admin's username (in any capitals) with "That username is taken.", as joining does for a space that exists. When setup finds one of those addresses taken already (the database wasn't quite empty), it uses a topic space there as it is and never a member's feed: a feed at `/c/general` is left as it was, not made essential or put on the default front page, and setup makes no General space.
80. **Who was told a space moved.** Migration 0027's notice that a topic space had moved aside (bullet 74) named the space, and went to its maker, the people its administer grant named and the admins without asking whether they could read it. Migration 0028 keeps each such notice only for people who can read the space it names, by the access layer's rule (members by the read grant; staff read every space until milestone 15; a suspension, being temporary, isn't counted), and deletes it for everyone else, and for anyone whose space can no longer be found at the address it gives. They aren't sent another notice: one naming no space would still tell them a space they can't see exists. (0027 itself couldn't be changed, since migrations are never edited once applied; 0028 runs straight after it, before the site serves anything.)

## Milestone 14, first step: per-thread permissions in the data and the access layer (migration 0029)

81. **Storing a restriction.** A thread's own `read` and `reply` restrictions are the four nullable arrays of §4: a part is `NULL` while the thread inherits its space's grant, and its group and user arrays are set or `NULL` together (a check constraint), as a `listed` grant's are. A conversation has none (another check), since its readers are its participants (§3.4). Beside the effective `visibility`, `threads.space_visibility` keeps the space's own (what `visibility` held until now): the access layer's one SQL condition then checks the space's grant and the thread's names without a join, and stays a function the planner inlines into plain column tests. `thread_readable` takes the thread's row (`thread_readable(t, vis, restricted_space_ids, reader, reader_groups)`); the reader is the viewer's id and groups, absent for visitors and suspended members, who are named by nothing. This adds a column to §4, which now lists it.
82. **Effective visibility.** A thread with a read restriction is `restricted`, whatever its space's grant and whoever it names, even if it names a group with every member in it: names are compared, not the people behind them (§3.1). A reply restriction alone leaves the visibility as the space's. Lifting the restriction gives the thread its space's visibility again. The trigger fires on inserts, moves and changes to the restriction, and the space's change of read grant reaches it as before, so a moved thread keeps its restriction, narrowed by its new space.
83. **Staff** still read every thread, restricted or not, until milestone 15 takes readership away from roles. They hold `reply` only where a grant names them, as §3.1 says of spaces: a thread's reply restriction holds them out like anyone else (they can still post in a locked thread they may reply in).
84. **Repliers must be readers** (§3.4): replying needs the space's `reply` grant, the thread's effective read audience and its own reply restriction, if any. Someone who can read but isn't named to reply is told "You can’t reply in this thread." where the reply box would be, and refused with it.
85. **Quote-forks from a restricted thread.** A thread's audience is its space's narrowed by its restriction (`Audience::narrowed`: in a public or members-only space that is just the names, since visitors and suspended members are never named; otherwise the intersection). §3.1's table is applied to each part: every part of the source must hold everyone in some part of the destination, comparing names. So a post in a thread restricted to group A can be quoted only into a space whose read grant names no more than A does, and no more than its own space allows. For intersections this can refuse a destination that no real person would see more through (the unit properties check that it never widens an audience, and that narrowing a source only ever takes destinations away), which is the conservative side §3.1 asks for. Moves aren't refused (staff move threads anywhere, as before); the moderation log's entry is public only if the thread's audience was public before and is public in its new space.
86. **Who changes a restriction.** The thread's author, while they can read it, and its space's administrators, staff included, whether or not they can read the thread, since §3.4 lets them widen it back to the space's audience. Every part of a restriction names the author too, as a feed's grants always name its owner (#70), so that nobody shuts the author out of their own thread; a restriction can only name groups, lists and people ("A thread can only be narrowed to groups, lists and people."). A suspended member changes nothing. A member of staff doing it in a space they don't run in their own right, to a thread that isn't theirs, is recorded in the moderation log ("changed who can read or reply in …", public only if the thread was and stays public). This step adds the operation (`writing::restrict_thread`) and its checks; the forms that call it come next, and will offer each person only the groups and lists they know, as space grant forms do (#52, #66, #67).
87. **"A feed with no threads"** (#71) now means no thread the viewer can read, since a feed whose only threads are restricted to Ruth's Friends would otherwise show everyone else a feed with nothing in it, telling them something is there. Its owner still always has it in their own sections. The cached pages (only visitors') change when a feed's public threads come or go, so the `site` tag is cleared when a feed with at most one public thread gains or loses a thread, or a thread there is restricted or opened (updating #72).
88. **Post counts.** The counts kept per member and space (migration 0019) count only threads that inherit their space's audience; a restricted thread's posts are counted per member and thread, and summed only for viewers who can read it, so the number beside a post or on a profile never counts posts the viewer can't see. Restricting a thread or lifting its restriction moves its counts at once (a trigger).
89. **Naming a list.** A group or list that a live thread's restriction names (#92) can't be deleted, as one a space's grant names can't (§3.3, a trigger, and the pages first): the list's page says "The list can’t be deleted while 1 thread names it." (or "2 spaces and 1 thread name it"), and the admin's group page "The group can’t be deleted while 1 thread names it. Their authors and the people who run their spaces can take it out."
90. **Left for the next steps of milestone 14**: the forms to set a restriction when starting a thread and on an existing thread, the lock and the named audience beside the title and the reply box when a thread is narrower than its space (§3.4, §5), the thread's audience page, and the browser tests for them (done: #96–#107).

### Corrections after review (migration 0030)

91. **A feed's owner is named in every restriction there.** §2 says the owner holds all of a feed's permissions, and #70 holds every grant to that; a thread restriction naming only its author would let a member who starts a thread in someone's guestbook shut the feed's owner out of it. So `writing::restrict_thread` names the feed's owner in each part, as it names the author, and a trigger (migration 0030) adds them to any restriction of a thread in a feed, however it comes there: restricted there, started there or moved in, by any path. It adds rather than refuses, as 0028's trigger does for grants, because staff may move a restricted thread into a feed and that must keep working. A thread moved out of a feed keeps the names it had, the owner's included, since nothing says whether they were named on purpose; that only keeps someone who could read it reading it. The property tests check after every step that a feed's owner can read and reply in every live thread in their feed.
92. **Deleted threads don't hold on to a list or group.** Nobody can change a deleted thread's restriction (#86 acts on live threads only), so counting deleted threads would leave a list undeletable until staff restored the thread, and its owner told that "1 thread names it" with nothing they or anyone could do. Only live threads now count, on the list's page, in the admin's group page and in the trigger that refuses the deletion (§3.3's "named in any grant" read as named by something live that can be changed). Deleting a group or list takes its id out of deleted threads' restrictions in the same statement, which only narrows them: a thread restored later is never wider than before, and is still restricted, to whatever names remain (perhaps only its author).
93. **What a restriction may name.** `writing::restrict_thread` checks the names itself, as a space's grant form does (#52, #66, #67), rather than leaving it to the forms that will call it: each part may name the site groups and the lists the editor owns or is on, and keep any other list that part names already (an administrator who doesn't know a list can keep it or take it out, never put it somewhere new), and may name members whose accounts aren't deleted, or keep people it names already. Anything else is refused with "Choose groups and lists from those offered." or "Name only people who are members here.". The database also refuses a restriction naming a group that doesn't exist (a trigger standing in for a foreign key on the arrays, which also keeps the group from being deleted at the same moment); a group deleted between the check and the write is refused with the first message.
94. **Cached pages and feeds visitors can't read.** #87's clearing of `site` when a feed's public threads come or go now also asks that the feed be readable by visitors: a members-only feed (the default) has no public threads, so every change in it was clearing `site`, which #72 meant to avoid. A feed's read grant changing clears `site` already, as any space's does.
95. **Merged restricted threads in the property tests.** The page machine sent the address of a merged thread on to the thread it was merged into whenever the viewer could read the old thread's space; the server (rightly) also asks that the old thread's restriction name them, since otherwise the redirect would tell them it existed. The model now asks the same.

## Milestone 14, the rest: the audience page, the lock and restricting a new thread (migration 0031)

96. **Who sees a thread's audience page.** `/t/{id}/audience` ("Who can take part in …") is for logged-in members who can read the thread, and for those who may change its restriction (#86): its author while they can read it, and the people who run its space, staff included, even when the restriction leaves them out. Someone who runs the space but can't read the thread is shown who it names, which they need in order to change it, but not its title: the page is "Who can take part in a thread in General", says that the thread doesn't let them read it, and has no link to it. Anyone else gets "not found", and visitors are asked to log in. What it says depends on who is looking, so it is never cached, like the space's audience page (#64).
97. **What the page says.** A panel for reading and one for replying. A part the thread inherits says so ("Everyone who can read General.", with a link to the space's audience page; "Everyone who can reply in General.", or "Everyone above who can reply in General." when only reading is narrowed). A restricted part says "Only these, of those who can read General:" (for replying, "of those who can read the thread and reply in General") and lists the groups and lists it names as the space's page does (#64): a site group as "Organisers group", a list the viewer owns or is on by name, each with how many are in it and who, a site group's members only to the people in it, and any other list as "People Ruth has chosen", once per owner; then the people it names one by one. A locked thread or an archived space says that only staff, or nobody, can post in it.
98. **The form.** For the thread's author and those who run its space, under the two panels: for reading, "Everyone who can read General" or "Only these people can read it:"; for replying, "Everyone who can read it and reply in General" or "Only these people can reply:"; each with the groups and lists a grant form would offer the editor for that part (#66, #67, the other lists the part already names as one "People Ruth has chosen" choice per owner) and members by username. The author, and a feed's owner, are always named (#86, #91) and aren't shown in the box; the hint says so ("You started it, so you always can.", "Ruth started it, so they always can. So can Maya, whose feed it is."). Saving goes back to the page with "Saved."; a problem shows the form again with what was typed. Choosing "Everyone who can read General" for both parts lifts the restriction: §3.4's widening back to the space's audience.
99. **Repliers are named among readers.** §3.4 says repliers must be readers, and the access layer already holds anyone named to reply but not to read out of the thread (#84). The form asks for it outright too: when both parts restrict, every group, list and person named to reply must be named to read as well, compared by names, as §3.1 compares audiences, rather than by who is in them, so that the answer doesn't change as memberships do and doesn't tell an editor who is on a list they don't know. `writing::restrict_thread` refuses anything else with "Only people who can read the thread can reply in it, so name each group, list and person who may reply among those who may read it too." Someone on a list that reads may be named to reply by also naming them to read, which changes nobody's access.
100. **The lock.** A thread narrower than its space shows, under its title and again just above the reply box (or the note saying the viewer can't reply), a ruled line with a small padlock: "Only Ruth’s Friends (12) and Ruth can read this thread.", "Only Ruth can reply.", or both, and a "Who can take part" link to its audience page. Who it names is said as everywhere else: site groups ("Organisers group", "Hosts and Organisers groups"), then lists as the viewer may know them (with how many are on them, or "people Ruth has chosen"), then people by display name. Each part is shown only when it narrows the space's grant for that verb, and that is decided by names, as §3.1 compares audiences (#99): a part narrows unless it names every group, list and person the space's grant names, so any restriction in a public or members-only space narrows, while a thread in a space only the Organisers group reads, restricted to the Organisers group and its author, does not, and shows no lock for that part, though it keeps its restriction and its audience page still says what it names. Comparing names rather than the people behind them keeps the lock from coming and going as memberships change, and from telling anyone who is on a list. A thread that inherits shows none of it (§3.4). Visitors never read a thread with a read restriction; a reply restriction alone isn't shown to them, since they can't reply anyway, which also keeps the names it gives out of their cached page.
101. **Thread lists** (the space page, `/latest` and its tabs, the index's latest threads) mark a thread whose own read restriction makes it narrower than its space (#100) with the same padlock and the same names, as a flag beside its title. A reply restriction alone isn't marked there: lists are about what the viewer can read, and the thread's page says who may reply.
102. **Reaching the form.** The thread page's toolbar has "Who can take part" for its author and those who run its space, whether or not the thread is restricted: it is a control for them, like "Moderate thread" for staff, not part of what the thread shows its readers. Everyone else reaches the page from the lock's link, which only a thread narrower than its space has.
103. **Restricting a new thread.** The new-thread form has "Who can read it": "Everyone who can read General" (the default) or "Only these people can read it:", with the same groups, lists and members by username as the audience page's form offers for reading, and the hint "Only those who can read General can read it, whoever you name. You always can, and you can change this later from the thread’s “Who can take part” page." It sets only reading, since replying is narrowed after, from the audience page. The thread is created and restricted in one transaction, before its notifications are queued, so nobody it leaves out is ever told about it or can see it. A new member's thread held for approval keeps the restriction (`held_posts.read_group_ids` and `read_user_ids`, migration 0031) and takes it when approved, less any group deleted while it waited, which only narrows it (so a held thread whose list was deleted is approved without it, rather than failing); the approval's log entry is public only if the thread is. The quote-fork form doesn't offer it: a fork's destination must already be at most as visible as its source (§3.6), and the new thread can be narrowed from its audience page straight after.
104. **The address.** `/t/{id}/audience` is a route of its own, so a thread whose title makes the slug `audience` gets `audience-thread`, as `unread`, `reply`, `subscribe` and `unsubscribe` do, and migration 0031 moved any existing one.
105. **The cache.** Changing a restriction from the form goes through `writing::restrict_thread`, which clears the thread's and its space's cached pages (and, for a feed, the site's when its public threads come or go, #87, #94), and records staff who don't run the space in their own right (#86). The lock never reaches a visitor's cached page (#100), so it needs no tags of its own.
106. **Property tests.** The access machine checks, for every viewer and every live thread in a space they read, that `content::thread_audience` says whether they read the thread (and only then gives its title) and names each part's groups and lists exactly as a space's audience page would and its people one by one; and its rule for restricting through the writing layer now expects a refusal when replying names more than reading. The page machine visits every thread's audience page (asked to log in, found for those who read it and for staff, not found otherwise) and checks that a thread's page has the lock exactly when its read restriction leaves out a group its space's read grant names (or its space isn't listed at all). Dropping the reply-within-read check fails the access machine at the thorough (CI) profile's 100 cases, though not always at the local 15.
107. **Browser test.** Each project's author is a seeded member of its own who logs in and posts little elsewhere (logins and posts are rate limited per account): the islands' members in the four projects without JavaScript, and one of each pair of correspondents in the two with it. They post in General rather than Board games, so as not to push Board games past one page of threads, which another test counts on.

## Milestone 15, first step: staff read only what their grants allow (migration 0032)

108. **Staff read like members.** `Viewer::filter` no longer gives staff `restricted`, `permissions::user_can_read` has no staff bypass, and `dm_readable` no longer opens a reported conversation to staff. A read grant whose level is `staff` still lets moderators and admins in, by their role: that is a grant naming them (§3.1), and `member_contexts.restricted_space_ids` keeps it. Like anyone, staff post only where they can read. Prototype decision 9 is superseded.
109. **Staff still run every space, readable or not.** `SpaceRules::may_administer` lets staff in whether or not they can read the space (members whom the administer grant names must still read it). A space's settings, archiving, deleting, the admins' list of spaces and the default front page's editor show staff every space by name, description and grants, which is what running it needs; never its threads (`content::administered_space`, `administered_spaces`). Its thread count for deletion counts threads without showing them.
110. **Moderating a thread one can't read.** `/mod/t/{id}` for a thread in a space the moderator can't read, or one whose restriction leaves them out, shows only what it is and where: its number, its space, who started it and when, how many posts, and whether it is locked, pinned or deleted, with the forms to lock, pin, rename (to a new title; the old one isn't shown), move, delete or restore it (`content::moderation::closed_thread`). The log names it to them as before only if they can read it. Choosing posts to move, and hiding or deleting a post, need reading it, so those stay with threads and posts the moderator can read; acting on a reported post they can't read comes with breaking glass on it (the next step).
111. **Interim refusals.** Two administer actions would let a moderator read what they can't, which §3.1 and §8 make a break-glass trigger: moving a thread they can't read into a space where they would read it, and changing the read grant of a space they can't read so that it lets them in (unless the administer grant names them, or a group or list of theirs; "staff" is the role again). Until the confirmation page exists both are refused, saying why ("You can’t read this thread, and moving it there would let you…", "You can’t read this space, and that would let you read it…"). Moving such a thread to another space they can't read either is allowed: that is how a space is folded into another. Adding themselves to a group or list is left to the step with the confirmation page. #122 adds a thread's restriction and closes a way round the space's.
112. **Reports carry no content.** A report on a post the moderator can't read is shown in the queue with what it was about and where (the space's name, or "A conversation you aren’t in"), who wrote it (with silence, suspend, suspend branch and delete as spammer, which act on the member), who reported it and why, and Dismiss, but not what it says. The report form for a message said "The moderators will see your report and who wrote this message, but not the conversation itself, which only the people in it can read." (replaced by #123). Opening reported content is breaking glass, in the next step.
113. **The grant tables.** As §4: `staff_access(id, actor_id, scope {thread, space}, thread_id?, space_id?, report_id?, reason, created_at, expires_at)`, a conversation being a thread, the scope's id required with it and the other absent, a reason of 1–500 characters, and `staff_access::open` setting the expiry 72 hours on (`staff_access::HOURS`); `staff_access_views(access_id, path, viewed_at)`. Both refer to their rows by foreign key (users are never hard-deleted). They are append-only: `UPDATE`, `DELETE` and `TRUNCATE` are revoked from `PUBLIC` and from the role that runs the migrations, which is the application's, and triggers refuse `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on every row, and `TRUNCATE` whenever the table holds anything (emptying an empty table removes nothing, and the development seed's reset of a database it made cascades through them). An empty space someone broke glass on can't be deleted, since the log names it: the store says so and suggests archiving.
114. **Grants are loaded with the member.** `member_contexts` gives the ids of the grants a member holds now (unexpired, and only while their role is moderator or admin, so that a moderator made a member loses them at once) and the thread or space each opens; `UserCtx.glass` holds them, with the page being rendered (path and query), which only the session middleware sets. `Viewer::filter` puts their threads and spaces in `ReadFilter` only for staff who aren't suspended, and only on a page: notification emails, jobs and the live stream's re-checks, which have no page, are opened nothing by a grant, so every read under one has a page to record.
115. **One way to read under a grant.** The SQL function `thread_granted(t, threads, spaces)` is the only place grants open anything, and only the content functions that serve a page of what a grant covers use it: `thread_page`, `space_page` with its list of threads, and `messages::conversation_page`; and `thread_slug`, for the redirect to a thread's page (which records the view when the address it gives shows the title, #121). Each of those records a `staff_access_views` row (one per grant covering what was shown, against the page asked for) before it returns, whenever a grant is what let the viewer in, and fails rather than return anything if no row was written. Everything else (the front page, `/latest`, search, notifications, emails, permalinks, histories, writing pages, audience pages, moderation pages) ignores grants. A grant on a space opens every thread in it, restricted ones included; a grant on a thread opens that thread's pages, not its space's page. A space's page records a view only when the grant shows something the viewer couldn't read anyway.
116. **Pages under a grant are for reading.** The thread page says "You can’t read this thread yourself. You can read it for now because you broke glass, and every page of it you open is recorded." and has no reply form, reactions or subscription toggle; the space page says the same of the space and offers no following, muting, marking read or audience link; a conversation says "You aren’t in this conversation. You can read it for now because you broke glass on it, and every page of it you open is recorded." Writing there (replying, reacting, subscribing) is refused as for anyone who can't read it. The notices to everyone who can read what a grant opens come with the next step.
117. **Held posts** (superseded by #119). Staff still see a held post's own text in the queue and on its page wherever it would go, since its author sent it to them for approval; the place is named without the title of a thread they can't read ("General › reply in a thread you can’t read", leading to its moderation page). A deleted thread's moderation page names the thread it was merged into only if the moderator can read that one.
118. **Property tests.** Both state machines' models give staff readership by membership only (the moderator viewer is in no group), and staff see every space's name. The page machine's new rule breaks glass on a thread, a space or a conversation (mostly on what the moderator can't read; some grants made already run out, by setting the trigger aside as the database's owner), and every page fetched as the moderator is checked to record views only against itself; text a grant opens may appear only on a page whose fetching recorded a view, and the pages of what a grant opens are found, and record one, exactly when the moderator couldn't read them otherwise. Other viewers' pages record nothing. Each test case's forum is emptied with the `TRUNCATE` trigger set aside. Skipping the recording in `thread_page` fails the moderator's suite at the local profile. The fork property and the permission models in `permissions.rs` lost the staff bypass.

## Milestone 15, first step, finished: what staff are shown on the way to breaking glass (migration 0033)

119. **Held posts are shown where staff could read them.** Principle 3 gives staff no readership by role, and a held post is content like any other: a moderator is shown what it says (in the queue, on its page `/held/{id}`, and its images) only where they could read it once it is up. That is the thread a reply would go in, or for a new thread its space and the read restriction its author chose (#103), decided by one SQL function, `held_readable` (migration 0033), which takes the viewer's parameters as `thread_readable` does. Anywhere else the queue and the page say "You can’t read where it would go, so what it says isn’t shown to you. You can still approve or reject it.", and name the place without a new thread's title: "Circle › new thread" (leading to the space's settings when they can't read the space, which they run), or "General › reply in a thread you can’t read" as before. Approving and rejecting need no reading, as moving and locking a thread don't (#110): they decide by who wrote it and where, and the post is then read like anything else there. Its author always sees their own. This replaces #117's first sentence: a new member sending a post for approval asks the moderators to approve it, not to read wherever it is going.
120. **An image's moderation page.** `/mod/uploads/{name}` names the posts that show an image (by number and thread title, leading to the post's moderation page) only in threads the moderator can read, and counts the others: "1 post in a thread you can’t read", "3 posts in threads you can’t read". Messages in conversations are counted as before. That is enough to decide whether to remove an image, which takes it out of every post without anyone reading them; `content::uploads::image_use` now takes the viewer, as every content read does.
121. **A redirect that shows a title is a page shown.** A thread's slug is made from its title, so the redirect from `/t/{id}` or from a wrong slug to `/t/{id}/{slug}` tells whoever follows it what the thread is called. Under a grant, `content::thread_slug` records a view against the address asked for before returning a slug the viewer's address didn't already hold; a redirect to another page of the thread, from an address that had the slug already, shows nothing new and records nothing, and the thread's page records its own view as before. This amends #115, which said the redirect shows nothing.
122. **Changing who may read, the interim refusals extended.** Lifting or widening a thread's restriction so that a moderator who can't read the thread could, or naming themselves in it, is refused like the moves and grant changes of #111 ("You can’t read this thread, and that would let you read it…"), unless they run its space in their own right: the administer grant names them by more than their role and they can read the space, which anyone else it names must do to run it (#109, `SpaceRules::runs_in_own_right`). Narrowing a restriction that still leaves them out is theirs to do. For a space's own read grant, #111 let off staff whom the administer grant names; but a moderator can name themselves there without being able to read the space (they run it by their role), and then open it. So that exception is gone: staff who can't read a space never change its read grant to let themselves in, however its administer grant reads.
123. **The report form says what opening it means.** For a post in a thread not every moderator can read (one whose audience is neither everyone, every member nor staff): "The moderators will see your report and who wrote this post. Not all of them can read this thread. If they need to open the thread to act on it, everyone who can read it will be told a moderator looked, but not who reported it or why." For a message: "The moderators will see your report and who wrote this message. Only the people in this conversation can read it. If they need to open the conversation to act on it, everyone in it will be told a moderator looked, but not who reported it or why. Your other conversations stay private." This is §8's wording, ahead of the confirmation page and its notices, which come with the next step; a post every moderator can read has no such note.
124. **Tests.** The integration tests (`tests/break_glass.rs`) cover each of #119–#123, the image page included. The access machine's held images are fetched by staff only where they could read the thread the held reply would go in, and the page machine shows a moderator a held thread's title and text only where they read its space; dropping the check from `upload_access` fails the access machine at 40 cases, so at the thorough (CI) profile, though not always at the local 15. The page machine now also checks every redirect's address for text the viewer may not see: dropping #121's recording from `thread_slug` fails the moderator's page machine at 40 cases too.
125. **The dev Postgres listens on port 25432.** The occasional test failure "unknown message type: '\0'" (prototype-decisions, known gaps) was a client connected to itself: the bytes sqlx read back were its own startup message. Port 54329 is in the ephemeral range of macOS (49152–65535) and Linux (32768–60999), and when the system gives a client that very port to connect from, TCP's simultaneous open joins the socket to itself. `dev/compose.yaml` now publishes Postgres on 25432, below every ephemeral range, and the README, `dev/check_builds.py` and the browser tests use it; `tests/dev_environment.rs` fails if any of them names a port in the range or one compose doesn't publish. 54329 stays published so that scripts still naming it keep working, with the old risk, until they move. CI's Postgres (5432) was never affected.

## Milestone 15, done: the confirmation page, the notices and the logs (migration 0034)

126. **The confirmation page.** `/mod/break-glass` takes one of `?report=` (a report on a post: its thread or conversation), `?thread=` (a thread or a conversation, by id) or `?space=` (by address), and a `POST` to the same address with a reason confirms. It is for staff only (anyone else is not found, as on the other moderation pages). What the viewer reads already leads straight there, and so does what a grant they hold now covers; anything else that exists and isn't deleted gets the page. Staff reach it from the queue ("Open…" on a report about something they can't read), from a thread's moderation page when they can't read the thread ("Read it…"), and from an unreadable space's settings ("Read it…"), which are the staff pages that know such a thing exists. Not-found pages offer nothing, so that a member of staff's 404 stays the same as anyone's; a conversation that wasn't reported is reached only by its id, as nothing in the staff pages names one.
127. **What the page says.** It names what would be opened by what it is and where, never its title or text: "thread № 12 in Circle, started by Kit on 3 Oct 2026", "a conversation between 2 people", "the whole space Circle" (every thread in it included); who will be told, as a count ("Everyone who can read the thread (3 people) will be told at once, on the site and by email, that a moderator (Sam) viewed it and when, and its pages will say so from then on. They won’t be told why, or who reported it."), or that nobody else can read it; that it lasts 72 hours, until when, with every page recorded; that the reason goes in the access log, which admins and everyone who can read what was opened can see, so not to name who reported it; and that no one else needs to approve it and it can't be taken back. The reason is required, 1–500 characters like every moderation reason. No second approver and no re-authentication, as §8 proposes.
128. **Changes that would let staff read are breaking glass.** The refusals of #111 and #122 become the confirmation page: moving a thread a moderator can't read into a space where they could, changing a space's read grant so that it lets them in, and lifting, widening or naming themselves in a thread's restriction answer with the page, which carries the form's fields and sends them back with `glass_reason`; once a reason will do, the change is made and a grant opened on the thread or space. Those who run the space in their own right still need none (#122). The list case of §8 is an admin adding themselves to a site group (`/admin/groups/{id}/members`) whose name in a read grant or a thread's restriction would let them read what they can't: the page lists each space and thread it would open, and confirming opens a grant on each. A member's own lists can't be the trigger: a list lets its owner in already, and only its owner changes it. Grants opened this way record no views, as the member of staff then reads by their memberships.
129. **Who is told.** Everyone who can read what is opened at the moment it is opened, as the access layer decides (`staff_access::readers`: `Viewer::filter` for each member, a suspended member reading only what visitors read), apart from the member of staff breaking glass; for a change of who may read, those who could read it before the change, whose privacy it touched. The grant and their notifications are written in one transaction. Other staff who can read it are told like anyone.
130. **The notice.** A notification of the new kind `staff_access`, which `notifications.staff_access_id` ties to its grant (migration 0034, which also lets it, like a note from the moderators, be about no post). It says "A moderator (Sam) viewed the thread “Brass” on 3 Oct 2026." ("your conversation “…”", "the space Circle"), with the moderator's display name and the day the grant was opened, never why or who reported it, and leads to the page of what was opened. Like a notification about a post, it is listed, counted on the badge and emailed only while its member can read what it names (`staff_access_readable`, which asks `thread_readable`, `dm_readable` and `space_readable`).
131. **Emailed at once.** §9 says break-glass notices are emailed at once, and §8 "under the normal rules": the email job runs straight away, whether or not the member has been on the site since, and the member's preference for "notes from the moderators" decides whether it is sent, as for any notice about them.
132. **The line on the page.** A thread's page shows "A moderator (Sam) viewed this thread on 3 Oct 2026." for each grant on that thread, a conversation's page the same for each on it, and a space's page for each on the whole space, to everyone shown the page, for as long as the row exists (forever, §4). A grant on a space isn't repeated on each thread page in it: its readers were each told, and the space's page says so. Opening a grant clears the cached pages of what it opened (`Change::StaffAccessOpened`), though visitors can't normally read anything a member of staff can't.
133. **Acting on a report under a grant.** While a grant covers a reported post's thread or conversation, the queue says so, links to it, and offers Hide post and Delete post, which `moderation::moderate_post` allows for a post the moderator can't read only under a running grant on its thread or space (asked with `thread_granted`, which shows nothing here and so records no view). The log entry is staff only. This is the "acting on a reported post they can't read comes with breaking glass" of #110.
134. **The admins' log.** `/admin/access` ("Break-glass log", in the admin navigation) lists every grant, newest first (the latest 500): who, what (its title only where the admin reads it themselves, and otherwise "№ 12 in Circle", "between 2 people" or the space's name, which staff know), whether a report led to it, why, when, whether it is still open, and how many pages were viewed under it. The pages' addresses aren't listed, as a thread's slug gives its title. Moderators who aren't admins don't see it.
135. **Each member's log.** `/settings/access`, "Moderators who looked", linked from the settings page, lists the grants on threads, conversations and spaces the member can read now, with who, what, when, why and how many pages, as §8's "the reason is in the log" and "visible … to each affected person" say together. That is why the confirmation page asks moderators not to name who reported it.
136. **The counts on `/moderation`.** The first page of the public log ends with "Moderators who read private content": a sentence on what breaking glass is, how many times it has happened in all, and a table of the last twelve months, months with none included. The page is cached as before, cleared by each new grant and expiring when a month begins. Breaking glass isn't also written to `mod_actions`: `staff_access` is its log.
137. **The privacy page.** Until an admin saves the terms and privacy pages, the privacy page has default text (`settings::DEFAULT_PRIVACY`), which the admin's form starts with: what Parlour does about moderators and private content, linking to `/settings/access` and `/moderation`, and §8's last paragraph, that whoever runs the server can read its database and Parlour can't keep them out. Whatever an admin saves, even nothing, replaces it.
138. **Tests.** `tests/break_glass.rs` covers opening a report (the queue's link, the page, a reason that won't do, the grant, its 72 hours, who is told and how, the page's line, the badge item, the emails sent at once, the logs, the counts, hiding the post under the grant, and closing again when it runs out), the staff pages' links, things read already, a space's and a thread's grant, each change that breaks glass once confirmed (and not before), and who counts as a reader; the crawl of every page a moderator holding grants can ask for now includes the new ones. The page machine's break-glass rule opens grants the way the page does and checks who is told against the reference model; every check looks for the line on each thread, space and conversation page exactly where a grant was ever opened, counts notices on the badge while their member reads what they name, and allows an email to name a space only to members who read it. It found that suspended members, who read what visitors read, are readers of public things. Dropping the space page's line fails the members' suite at the local profile. A browser test, `e2e/tests/break-glass.spec.ts`, reports a post in a thread only two can read, breaks glass on it from the queue, and finds the line, the notifications and the log for both readers, with JavaScript and without; the seed has, for each browser project, a pair of established members with a thread of their own in General and a moderator, so that no other test's rate limits are shared.
139. **Prototype decision 9** now points at this: a report opens nothing, and a moderator opening it breaks glass for 72 hours, logged and announced.
