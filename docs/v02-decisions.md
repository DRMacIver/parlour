# Decisions made while building v0.2

Where DESIGN.md v0.2 was silent or ambiguous, the build agents chose the option closest to it and recorded the choice here, one numbered bullet per choice, so it can be confirmed or changed. Deviations from DESIGN.md are marked as such and noted in DESIGN.md too. Milestones 9 onwards.

## Milestone 10, first step: layouts, the default, per-member copies (migrations 0023 and 0024)

1. **Spaces outside any section** at migration time went into a section called "Spaces", first in the default layout, since that is where the old index showed them. It only exists on forums that had such spaces.
2. **The "Feeds" section is known by its name.** A member's copy of the default keeps the member's own section named "Feeds" (lowest position if there are several), with what is in it, and puts it last. The copy skips default sections named "Feeds" and instead adds whatever they hold to the member's Feeds section. A migrated default has no Feeds section; the setup wizard's does, empty, as §3.5 says.
3. **"Members who haven't edited theirs"** are those whose layout has `edited_at` null. Every change to the default copies it again into those layouts (`layout_copy_default` in the migration, called from `crate::layouts` after each default write). Adding, renaming, moving or deleting a section, and following, unfollowing or moving a space, marks a member's layout as edited; muting does not, so a member who only mutes keeps following the default.
4. **A space is in at most one section of a layout** (a trigger enforces it). Following a space that is already in another section of the layout moves it there.
5. **Essential spaces** are shown in one block at the top of the front page, headed "Spaces", in their order on the viewer's layout and then by name, and are left out of the sections below so nothing appears twice. Sections with nothing to show (empty, or holding only spaces the viewer can't read, has muted, or that are archived) are not rendered. An empty front page says so and links to `/spaces`.
6. **Muting** takes the space out of the member's sections, as §3.5 says, whether or not they have edited their layout, and a later copy of the default into an unedited layout leaves the muted space out (migration 0024; the first version of the copy put it back, hidden only by the front page's filter). Unmuting does not put a space back in any section, whether or not the member follows the default: following it does, and following a muted space unmutes it. (A layout that follows the default is the default less what its owner has muted now, so a member who follows the default and unmutes a space does see it again the next time the default changes; one who has edited theirs doesn't.) Making a space essential unmutes it for everyone; the store and a trigger both refuse to mute an essential space.
7. **Positions.** Sections keep whatever position they are given and sort by `(position, id)`, so an editor can number them as it likes; a space is placed at an index within its section and the section's spaces are renumbered from 0. New sections and followed spaces go last.
8. **Setup's first spaces.** Announcements and General are readable by everyone (a forum's front door should have something on it; §3.1's members-only default for *new topic spaces* is left as it is), replied to by members, started by staff (Announcements) or members (General), administered by staff rather than by the first admin personally, created by the admin, and essential. If a slug is already taken (the database wasn't quite empty), that space is used instead of making one.
9. **The admin pages.** `/admin/spaces` lists spaces by the default layout's sections, then "Not on the default front page", marking essential and archived ones, and keeps its forms for adding, renaming, moving and deleting the default layout's sections at `/admin/sections…` until `/admin/layout` arrives in the next step. A space's settings page gives staff a "Section on the default front page" select ("Not on it" takes the space off; choosing a section appends it there; the old position field is gone, ordering being the layout editor's job) and admins an "Essential" box. Staff doing either to a space they don't administer in their own right is logged (`edit_space`, `mark_space_essential`, `unmark_space_essential`).
10. **Breadcrumbs** on space and thread pages no longer name a section, since sections are each member's own.
11. **Deleting an account** deletes the member's layout and mutes along with everything else that is theirs alone.
12. **The default layout row is made on demand** if missing (tests empty the tables with `TRUNCATE … CASCADE`); a partial unique index keeps there from being two. Writers to a layout take its row lock, and copying the default into a member's layout takes that layout's lock too, so two admins saving at once (or an admin's save and a member's edit) don't trip over each other's copying; a new account's copy waits for any change to the default in progress. Propagating a change to the default lists the followers once and then, for each, locks their row and checks again that `edited_at` is still null (migration 0024): a member's first edit committed while the propagation waited for their row is kept, and their layout is theirs from then on.
13. **Tests.** The fixture's `Forum::space` puts each space in a default-layout section (the one given, or a catch-all "Spaces" section that comes first) so that existing tests about the front page keep meaning what they did; `Forum::unlisted_space` makes a space on nobody's front page. The access-layer property model tracks each member's layout, mutes and the essential flag, and checks the front page against them.
14. **The data export** (§2) includes the member's front page as they have arranged it, under `front_page` (`edited_at`, and the sections in order, each with the slugs of its spaces, whether or not they can read them now, since the arrangement is theirs), and the slugs of the spaces they have muted under `muted_spaces`.
15. **The moderation log** says "made Games essential" and "made Games no longer essential" for the two essential actions, the space's name before the complement, as a member would say it.
16. **Left for later steps** of milestone 10, after the second step below: `/settings/layout` and the editor island with its plain form, and `/admin/layout`.

## Milestone 10, second step: the `/latest` tabs, unread counts, mutes, Follow and Mute forms

17. **The Front page tab** (`/latest/front`) lists the threads in every space the front page shows: the essential spaces and the spaces in the viewer's sections. §3.5 said "every space in your sections"; essential spaces are on the front page whatever the sections hold, so a tab called "Front page" without them would be odd (the setup wizard's forum would have an empty one). §3.5 now says so. Archived spaces are left out, as the front page leaves them out; All keeps them, as it did.
18. **A section's tab** (`/latest/s/{id}`) lists the threads in that section's spaces as the layout has them, essential spaces included, readable, not archived and not muted. Tabs are shown for All, Front page (members only) and every section with at least one space the viewer can see, plus the section being looked at (its page works even when there is nothing to show). The section must belong to the viewer's layout (the default layout's sections for a visitor); anyone else's, or nonsense, is not found. A visitor asking for `/latest/front` is sent to `/latest`, since they have no front page of their own and the design gives them only All and the default's sections. Each tab has one address (`?page=1` and padded ids redirect), so visitors' tabs stay in the anonymous cache under bounded keys, tagged with the spaces they cover and `site` (the default layout).
19. **Mutes** leave a space out of the All tab (and so the index's "Latest" list), the Front page and section tabs, the default view of `/spaces`, and search results, unless the search names that space with `in=`, in which case the member has asked for it. `/spaces?muted=1` shows a member's muted spaces, flagged "Muted"; the link to it appears once they have muted something, and for visitors `muted=1` is just another spelling of `/spaces`. The muted space's own page says it is muted and what that means. The All tab's note says it leaves out the spaces the member has muted, when they have.
20. **Follow, Unfollow, Mute and Unmute** are plain forms in the space page's toolbar, for members who can read the space. Follow puts the space at the end of the first section of the member's layout (by position, whatever it is called, Feeds included), or makes a section called "Following" when the layout has none; the toolbar then says which section it is in. Following and unfollowing mark the layout edited, as any arrangement does (bullet 3); muting doesn't. An essential space offers no Mute and refuses one with a 403. A suspended member can do none of these; a visitor is sent to log in.
21. **Unread counts** on the front page count a space's threads with posts past the member's read marker (a thread never opened counts, as prototype decision 10 says), shown as "N unread" beside the space's name; visitors see none.
22. **"Mark all read" per section** is `POST /latest/s/{id}/read`, offered on the section's tab and in the section's heading on the front page (whose form says `next=/` to come back there). It moves the member's read markers to the end of every thread they can read in the section's spaces that the tab would show. It works only on the member's own sections.
23. **Query budgets.** A visitor's `/latest` is now four queries instead of three: the default layout's sections make the tabs. A member's stays at five: whether they have muted anything comes back with their sections.
24. **Wording.** The watch form's "of nothing (muted)" became "of nothing", so that "mute" means one thing, the per-space mute, and the notifications page's foot says "Choose how closely to watch a thread or space from its page".
25. **A member's layout writes never reach the default.** Follow and Unfollow find the member's layout with `layouts::own_id`, which makes them a copy of the default first if they somehow have none (every account is given one at creation, so this is belt and braces), rather than with `layouts::id_for`, whose fallback to the default is for reading a front page and would otherwise have put a member's change into the default and, from there, into every follower's front page. `id_for` is documented as read-only.
