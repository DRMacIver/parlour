# Notes on the v0.2 build

The prototype was taken from DESIGN.md v0.1 to v0.2 by an automated workflow, in milestones 9–16 (each one implemented, reviewed and fixed, then a review of the whole build and its fixes). Wherever DESIGN.md was silent or ambiguous, the build agents chose the option closest to it and recorded the choice, one numbered bullet each, in `docs/v02-decisions.md`; the commits from `37b0f4b` (milestone 9) to `57980d8` say what each step did. This file collects what you should look at: the choices to confirm, where the code and DESIGN.md differ, and what was left out. Numbers like #66 are bullets in `docs/v02-decisions.md`.

## Open questions

These are choices the build made that you should confirm or change. Most are easy to change; the ones that are not say so.

1. **The setup wizard's spaces are public.** *Resolved (6 October 2026): they are members-only, and visitors get an admin-written welcome instead (#165).* Announcements and General were readable by visitors, though §3.1 makes "members" the default for a new topic space and a public space "an explicit choice" (#8). The reasoning was that a forum's front door should have something on it.
2. **Essential spaces on the front page.** They are shown once, in a block headed "Spaces" at the top, and left out of the sections below (#5). So on a new forum the setup wizard's "Forum" section, which holds only Announcements and General, never shows: everything in it is already at the top. The "Front page" tab of `/latest` includes the essential spaces (#17).
3. **Following and muting the default.** A member "has edited" their layout once they add, rename, move or delete a section, or follow, unfollow or move a space; from then on changes to the default no longer reach them. Muting is not an edit (#3). Unmuting doesn't put a space back in a section, and following a muted space unmutes it (#6). Follow puts a space at the end of the first section, whatever it is called, or makes a "Following" section (#20). The "Feeds" section is recognised by its name (#2).
4. **Unread counts.** A thread a member has never opened counts as unread on the front page, as prototype decision 10 already does for thread lists (#21).
5. **What the notifications migration kept.** Old `reply`, `watched_thread` and `watched_space` notifications became `subscription` rather than being deleted; thread watches at "normal" or "muted" were dropped; space watches were dropped, with a one-time in-app notice that is not emailed (#37, #39, #40). Quoting someone no longer notifies them; mentioning them does (#36).
6. **Conversations use subscriptions.** Everyone in a conversation is subscribed to it; the old Mute is now "Turn off notifications", so that "mute" means only the per-space mute (#38, #24).
7. **Role changes notify the member** ("An admin made you a moderator.") (#45). §9 lists role changes among the notices; the wording is new.
8. **Who may name a list in a grant.** Anyone on a list may name it, not only its owner (#66). The build first allowed only the owner, then reversed this: §3.3 says the owner alone decides who is *on* a list, and naming it in a grant changes nobody's membership. The cost falls on the owner, who can't delete a list that a grant names, possibly in a space they can't read (the page tells them how many, and that only those spaces' administrators can take it out, #56). Not easy to reverse once lists are in use.
9. **A list lets its owner in**, and the owner is never one of its members, so they can't add themselves (#49).
10. **Site groups' members are shown only to people in the group**, on audience pages (#64). §3.3 says site groups are visible to all members, which the build read as their names, not their membership.
11. **No "leave a list".** Only the owner removes people, and nobody is told they were added or removed (#54, #55). Someone who doesn't want to be on a list has no way off it.
12. **What someone removed from a list keeps.** Built as §3.3's main text: they keep each of their own posts' history and edit form, showing that post alone. This holds however they lost the thread (taken off a list or group, a grant changed, the thread moved), for live posts in spaces, not conversations, and not while suspended (#58).
13. **Feeds' addresses and names.** A feed is at the username in lower case, and may hold underscores where topic spaces can't (#69). Its name follows the owner's display name only while the two are the same, so a renamed feed keeps its name. The migration moved topic spaces whose address was a member's username to an address ending "-space" and told the people who could read them (#74, #80).
14. **"A feed with no threads"** means no thread *the viewer* can read, so a feed whose only threads are restricted to Ruth's Friends looks empty to everyone else (#87). Following a feed with no threads isn't refused (#71).
15. **Feeds are permanent.** A feed can't be deleted, even empty, and deleting its owner's account archives it for good under "Deleted member" (#75, #76).
16. **Restrictions always name the author, and a feed's owner** (#86, #91). A thread with any read restriction is `restricted`, even if it names a group containing every member: names are compared, not people (#82). Repliers must be named among readers by name, not by who is in each group (#99).
17. **When the lock shows.** A part of a restriction is shown as a lock only if it narrows the space's grant *by names*: a thread in an Organisers-only space restricted to Organisers and its author shows no lock (#100). Thread lists flag only read restrictions (#101).
18. **Quote-forks from restricted threads** apply §3.1's table to each part of the intersection of the space's grant and the thread's restriction. This is conservative: it can refuse destinations no real person would see more through (#85).
19. **A new thread can be given only a read restriction**, not a reply one, and the quote-fork form offers neither; both can be set from the thread's audience page straight after (#103).
20. **What staff see of what they can't read.** Staff run every space, so they see every space's name, description and grants, but never its threads (#109). A thread they can't read shows them its number, space, author, date, size and state on `/mod/t/{id}`, with the forms to lock, pin, rename, move, delete or restore it (#110). A post held for approval is shown to them only where they could read it once it is up, though they can approve or reject it either way (#119).
21. **Break glass: who is told, and what they see.** The confirmation page asks for a reason, which everyone told can read in their own log (#127, #135); so the page asks moderators not to name the reporter, but nothing enforces that. For a change that would let staff read (a grant, a restriction, a move, joining a group), those told are the people who could read it *before* the change, including those it shuts out (#129, #130). The notice names what they can no longer read without its title (#140). A suspended member is told as a reader (#147).
22. **Break glass by a change lasts as long as the change.** A grant opened by a change of who may read isn't recorded page by page: the member of staff then reads as an ordinary reader, for as long as the change stands, not for 72 hours (#128, #146).
23. **Break-glass emails honour the preference.** They are sent at once, but only to members who allow emails for "notes from the moderators" (#131). The other option is to send them whatever the preference says.
24. **Moving a thread away from its readers breaks glass** for staff who can't read it, even when they never could read it afterwards, because it is a first step to reading it (#145). Staff named in a space's administer grant still break glass to read its restricted threads (#143).
25. **Those who run a space moderate it** (#142): administrators and feed owners pin, lock, rename and delete threads, hide and delete posts, and move threads to another space they run. They can't undo what staff did, and their hide still shows as "Post hidden by a moderator". Restoring deleted threads, moving posts, reports and everything about members stay with staff.
26. **The public log and private content.** `/moderation` lists actions on non-public content too, naming the account and the action but with no reason and no title the viewer can't read (#149), and ends with a count of break-glass accesses for the last twelve months (#136). The privacy page has default text about staff and private content until an admin saves their own (#137).
27. **Handing on a space nobody runs** is done by staff from its settings, once its maker has been away 60 days (#151). A member asks for one by writing to the moderators; there is no form for it.
28. **Rate limit on lists:** 20 at once, then one every five minutes (#59).
29. **Deployment defaults.** The pre-migration dump is taken by a compose service each time the stack comes up, not by the binary (#160). Nightly backups run at 03:30 UTC and are kept 14 days; pre-migration dumps, the newest five (#161).
30. **The old dev port.** Postgres is published on 25432 and also still on 54329, which is in the ephemeral range and can make a client connect to itself (#125). Scripts still naming 54329 should move, and then it should go.

## Deviations from DESIGN.md

The code differs from the design text in these places. Where the change was deliberate it is in `docs/v02-decisions.md`, and most are now noted in DESIGN.md too.

- **§3.1, space slugs and feeds.** A feed's slug is the username *in lower case*, and may hold underscores, which no topic space's slug may (#69). DESIGN.md now says so.
- **§3.1, staff and the administer grant.** "A space whose `administer` grant is empty … is administrable by site staff": staff administer every space anyway. What counts as a space "nobody runs" for the 60-day claim is broader: a grant whose named people, groups and lists let in nobody whose account still exists (#151).
- **§3.1, staff naming themselves.** §3.1 says staff actions in a space they don't administer "in their own right" are logged. The build defines that once (`SpaceRules::runs_in_own_right`): named by more than their role, and able to read the space. A space whose administer grant is "staff" (Announcements and General, and every space from before milestone 9) is run by role, so every staff change there is logged (#144).
- **§3.2, a feed's name.** "Its name is the owner's display name": it follows the display name only until the owner renames the feed (#69). "A feed with no threads" is read per viewer (#87).
- **§3.3, how lists are shown.** There is no "Visible to: alice's Friends (12)" line. Lists appear in audience labels wherever a space's audience is shown ("Ruth’s Friends (12)", or "people Ruth has chosen"), on the space's and thread's audience pages, and in a thread's lock (#51, #64, #100). Site groups' members are listed only to the people in them (#64).
- **§3.4, the lock's position.** "Next to the title and next to the reply box": it is a ruled line under the title and just above the reply box (#100).
- **§3.5, the setup wizard's default layout.** "A section 'Forum' with Announcements and General": they are there, but, being essential, they are shown in the essential block at the top and the Forum section shows as empty (open question 2).
- **§4, data model.** Some columns aren't in §4's listing: `spaces.visibility`, kept from the read grant as `threads.visibility` is (migration 0022); `space_grants.level` also allows `staff` (§3.1 describes it, §4 omits it); `held_posts.read_group_ids` and `read_user_ids`, the restriction a held new thread will get (migration 0031, #103); and `user_space_post_counts.thread_id`, so a restricted thread's posts are counted only for its readers (migration 0029, #88). `users.last_seen_at`, from v0.1, now also decides the 60-day claim (#151). DESIGN.md §4 now lists the first two.
- **§5, cache tags.** Besides `thread:N`, `space:N`, `user:N` and `site`, there are tags for the members' list and invite tree, each invite's page and the public moderation log. A feed's public threads coming or going clears `site` only when the feed has at most one (#72, #87, #94). Opening a break-glass grant clears the pages of what it opened (#132).
- **§6, the access layer.** `UserCtx` also carries the member's running break-glass grants and the page being rendered (#114). Grants open a post's history, permalink and moderation page and images, not only the thread, space and conversation pages (#148). DESIGN.md says so.
- **§8, the list trigger.** "Changing … a list's membership in a way that would include themselves": a member's own lists can't do it (a list lets in only its owner and the people the owner adds), so the trigger is an admin adding themselves to a *site group* named in a grant or restriction (#128). DESIGN.md §8 now says so.
- **§8, hiding.** "Hide post, which keeps it for staff": also for those who run the post's space (#142).
- **§8, who is told.** "Everyone who can read the scope": for a change of who may read, everyone who could read it *before* the change (#129). "Each affected person, for their own threads and spaces": each member's log lists the grants they were told about, whether or not they can read what they name now (#135).
- **§8, the queue.** "The content appears inline only if the staff member can already read it": or while a running grant covers it, which also lets them hide or delete it from the queue (#133).
- **§9, email for break-glass notices.** Sent at once, whether the member has been on the site or not, but subject to their preference for notes from the moderators (#131).
- **§9, notices.** Besides those listed, members are told when a space is handed to them (#151) and, once, when a space they could read was moved aside for a feed (#74).
- **§10, the pre-migration dump** is taken by the compose file's `pre-migration-dump` service, not by the app before it migrates (#160). DESIGN.md says so. The Dockerfile is at the repository's root, not in `deploy/` (§12 says so).
- **§11, URLs.** `POST /c/{slug}/read` marks every thread in a space read (§3.5's "Mark all read … per space"); it was missing from the table and is now in it.
- **§12, "CI runs all of them".** The workflow runs the Rust tests (Hegel picks its thorough `ci` profile there), the islands' tests and the browser tests, but it has never run, since the repository has no remote; and it doesn't build the Docker image.

## Known gaps

- **CI has never run** (there is no remote), and it doesn't build the image. The image and the deployment stack were checked by hand once (#163).
- **Some property-test mutations are caught only at the thorough profile.** Dropping the reply-within-read check, the held-image check or the redirect's view recording fails at the `ci` profile's 100 cases but not always at the local 15 (#106, #124). The suite takes several minutes locally.
- **The append-only log** is protected by revoked privileges and triggers, but the application's role owns the tables, so someone deliberately altering the database's definitions could get round it (#157). Run Parlour as a role that isn't a superuser, as the compose file does.
- **Grants opened before migration 0036** can't be told apart from reading grants, so a few made by a change show in the logs as if they were 72-hour, recorded grants (#146).
- **Frozen lists** (whose owner's account was deleted) can't be renamed or deleted by anyone, and nobody can change who is on them (#57).
- **No member-facing claim form** for a space nobody runs, and no "leave a list" (open questions 11 and 27).
- **Restricting from the fork form.** A quote-fork can't be restricted when it is made, and a new thread can be given only a read restriction there (#103).
- **Moving posts out of a thread a moderator can't read** isn't possible without breaking glass on it, since choosing them needs reading them (#110).
- **The migration notices** about spaces moved aside (0027) and about notifications changing (0025) have no moderation-log entry, since a migration has no actor; the notice is the record (#40, #74).
- **From the prototype, still open:** no admin view of failed or queued jobs; no cleanup of unused uploads or old notifications; times are shown in UTC with no time-zone setting; search uses Postgres's `english` configuration (prototype decisions, known gaps and open questions 11–12).
- **The "soon after" list** in DESIGN.md §2 is untouched: open registration, passkeys and SSO, rule-based sections, nested groups, a gate on space creation, digests, Atom feeds, webhooks, bookmarks, web push, importers and the rest.
